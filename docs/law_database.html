Laws Directly Governing Routing Apps

Definition: The statutory or regulatory text explicitly names navigation apps, GPS routing, routing providers, digital mapping services, or their algorithmic route outputs, imposing an affirmative obligation, reporting charter, disclaimer mandate, or direct civil liability on them.

Canonical Topics & Benchmark Instruments:
  • • Topic 1.1 Slow Streets Exclusion: California AB 2015 (Enacted Ch. 207, Stats. 2026; sunset Jan 1, 2033; empowers localities to exclude designated slow streets).
  • • Topic 1.2 Emergency Rerouting: New Jersey A3036 SAFER APP (Enacted Jan 20, 2026 as P.L. 2026, c. 12; Attorney General emergency rerouting orders; up to $1,000 fine).
  • • Topic 1.3 Heavy Vehicle Route Rules: Connecticut HB 5071 & HB 6709 (Failed at end of 2025 session); Massachusetts HD.4949 (Stalled in House Rules).
  • • Topic 1.4 Bridge Clearance Mandates: New York S6197 / A3536 (Pending in Senate May 2026); NY A8582 (Failed); NY A4167 & A5717 (Pending commercial GPS); Federal H.R. 6531 (Bridges Not Bumpers Act of 2025).
  • • Topic 1.5 Study & Data Reporting: California AB 2015 (Enacted; Caltrans statewide empirical study due Jan 1, 2028); Federal MAP Roads Act (Pending rural road digitization).

Directly Applicable Cross-Sector Laws

  • • Topic 2.7 Commercial Routing Standards: 49 CFR Part 397 (Hazmat corridors); 23 CFR Part 658 (National Network & 1-mile access rule); 23 U.S.C. § 127 (Interstate weight limits).

Indirectly Applicable Road & Traffic Laws

Definition: Laws governing physical road use, intersection turn restrictions, street closures, modal filters, speed limits, parking permits, and tolling. Navigation apps are not named, but act as passive cost-distance calculators that must route around these physical constraints.

Canonical Topics & Benchmark Instruments:
  • • Topic 3.1 Closures & 3.2 Turn Bans: California CVC § 21101 (Local police powers); CVC §§ 22101 & 22113 (Turn signs); LAMC § 80.14 peak turn bans.
  • • Topic 3.3 Parking & 3.4 Truck Routes: CVC § 22507 (Preferential parking); CVC § 22507.5 & § 35701 (Commercial truck exclusions); Texas Ch. 621–623; 58 CA County truck codes.
  • • Topic 3.5 Automated Cameras: Arkansas Act 707 & §§ 27-52-110/111; WA RCW 46.63.220; NY VTL § 1180-b NYC school cameras; Alabama SB 341 (Act 2026-475); Texas Ch. 707 ban; CT PA 23-116 & PA 26-14; Arizona Proposition 319 voter-approved photo radar ban.
  • • Topic 3.6 Pricing & 3.7 HOV/HOT: 23 U.S.C. § 129 (Federal-aid tolling conditions); 23 U.S.C. § 166 (HOV occupancy & HOT dynamically priced lanes).
  • • Topic 3.8 Planning & 3.9 MUTCD: 23 U.S.C. § 149 (CMAQ); Pub. L. 117-58 (IIJA transportation authorization); 23 CFR Part 655 / Federal MUTCD 11th Edition.

Model 2: Delegated Closed-List / Preemption

State Preemption & Enumerated Powers

States where local traffic regulation is preempted unless the municipality fits strictly within an enumerated statutory subdivision under strict judicial construction.

Model 3: Centralized State Approval

Mandatory State DOT Pre-Approval

Municipalities cannot alter traffic circulation or close streets connecting to state routes without prior formal review and written approval by the State DOT Commissioner.

• New Jersey: N.J.S.A. § 39:4-8 & § 39:4-197
• Precedent: Rosa v. Borough of Leonia (2018)
Unilateral local permit bans held ultra vires without State Commissioner sign-off.

Regulatory Lag: 20th-Century Physical Codes vs. 21st-Century Algorithmic Navigation

1959 — 2026

A core finding of this legal analysis is the chronological divergence between statutory architecture and digital navigation. Most state vehicle codes governing street access were enacted between 1959 and 1982 to address static, physical congestion. In contrast, modern third-party routing applications recalculate individual-optimal travel times dynamically every 30 seconds, rerouting hundreds of vehicles onto local streets.

The Pre-Digital Legal Baseline (1959–1991)
  • • Cal. Veh. Code § 35701 (1959): Focuses entirely on gross vehicle weight (curbing heavy trucks on local streets to protect pavement).
  • • City of Lafayette (1979) & CVC § 21101.6: Preempted selective gates and resident-only street closures, barring cities from closing roads to through commuters.
  • • Rumford v. City of Berkeley (1982): Preempted municipal physical diverters, establishing that the State owns all public streets.
  • • City of Poway (1991) (229 Cal. App. 3d 847): Banned closing roads connecting adjoining municipalities to prevent regional traffic dumping.
The Algorithmic Response Era (2014–2026)
  • • 2014–2018 Municipal Turn Bans: Cities adapt § 21101(f) to erect peak-hour turn signs (LAMC § 80.14) to break navigation app routing algorithms.
  • • 2018 Leonia Permit Ban (Rosa v. Leonia): Attempt to close 60 residential streets to non-residents struck down under pre-digital Title 39 statutory limits.
  • • 2026 California AB 2015 (Ch. 207): The first state statute specifically mandating a comprehensive pilot study to modernize the regulatory framework for routing applications (Sts. & Hy. Code § 155.8).
