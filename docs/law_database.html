Core framework

Six-category codebook

The three tiers describe the legal relationship to routing apps. Categories A–F describe the regulatory mechanism. Each retained instrument receives one primary tier and one supported primary subclass; secondary codes are stated in its notes. Category E alone does not establish Tier 1.

The complete framework contains six categories and 35 subclasses. All definitions remain visible even when no checked entry currently uses a subclass. Counts reflect the complete 3,455-record codified collection.

Interpretation

Legal & policy analysis

These tables describe the complete nationwide screening of 3,455 codified legal instruments across all jurisdictions, instrument types, and regulatory categories.

Different legal subjects, different effects

Tier 1 requires express provider-facing operative language. Tier 2 instruments can affect route availability, eligibility, costs or road information through other actors; they do not establish a provider duty merely by mentioning traffic. Tier 3 addresses qualifying business conduct or liability outside the road-intervention framework.

Authority is different from implementation

An enabling law does not prove that a particular local restriction, camera, permit program or rerouting directive exists. Each record states the relevant scope, exceptions and source currency. No causal policy-effectiveness conclusion is drawn from the number of legal entries.
