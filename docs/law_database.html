Architecture

Routing App Legal Regulation Framework: Three-Tier Architecture

Layers 1 and 2 address the AB 2015 research scope. Layer 1 concerns routing app providers as legal subjects. Layer 2 concerns other principal subjects with indirect effects on routing apps. Layer 3 covers other applicable laws. Overview counts and primary breakdowns now use all 3,455 source records in the content-review cohort. The earlier index is retained separately.

The database aims to cover all legislation and regulations that directly or indirectly affect routing apps or the routes they recommend at the U.S. federal level, at the state level in all 50 states, and at every level in California, including county and municipal ordinances.

All 3,455 source records are listed in the layer groups below, with principal subjects, content evidence and classification reasons. Unresolved records include missing bill text, uncertain scope and proposed exclusions. Download the updated record-level classification.

How each record receives its final layer
  1. Open the official operative text and confirm the instrument, jurisdiction, session and legal status. Exclude non-binding or irrelevant material and connect duplicate or amended versions.
  2. Identify the principal legal subject and the actual right, duty, authority, remedy or exemption. A reference to GPS or a map alone does not establish that a routing app provider is regulated.
  3. Within the AB 2015 research scope, assign Layer 1 when the provision expressly makes routing apps or their providers a legal subject; assign Layer 2 when it principally governs another actor and indirectly affects routing apps or their recommended routes.
  4. Assign Layer 3 to other applicable law outside those first two groups. General privacy, consumer-protection and platform-liability provisions require a separate applicability analysis.
  5. Record the citation, operative passage and classification rationale. Where a record spans layers, use the most direct relationship as primary and note the other layer. Leave the classification unresolved when the evidence is insufficient. Assign the six-category code independently of the layer.

AB 2015 itself belongs in Layer 2 under this framework because its research and reporting requirements address Caltrans. H.R. 6531 is a reviewed Layer 1 proposal because the introduced text expressly addresses GPS-administrator immunity. A proposed right or duty remains a proposal until enactment is confirmed. These examples explain the rule; they are not additional records in the pending count.

Road access and vehicle eligibility

Federal access protections
23 CFR § 658.19 addresses reasonable access for covered vehicles between the National Network and specified destinations. The rule distinguishes access circumstances and provides for safety restrictions. A truck route analysis needs the vehicle class, destination and any applicable access exception. Federal rule.
State limits and clearances
Minnesota and Ohio set vehicle dimension limits with qualifications by vehicle type or use. Washington separately addresses impaired overhead clearance. Route eligibility depends on both vehicle dimensions and road-specific information. Minnesota, Ohio and Washington.
Delegation to local governments
Virginia provides a process for restricting through-truck traffic on certain highways. The relevant route facts include the affected road segment, applicability to through traffic, and any alternative route designated under the process. Virginia Code § 46.2-809.
State review of local restrictions
In the Leonia trial-court dispute, the resident-access ordinances affected a state road, triggering the state approval issue. The court denied reconsideration and a stay of its earlier summary judgment. New Jersey court opinion.

Direct app regulation and legislative proposals

New Jersey's SAFER APP Act establishes a direct mechanism for ongoing emergency traffic conditions attributable to turn-by-turn routing guidance. After the statutory determination, the Attorney General can order rerouting or consult with the Commissioner of Transportation and engage the operator on an alternative. This tool differs from the state approval process for local ordinances illustrated by Leonia. Final bill text and enactment record.

Existing clearance and access rules govern vehicle movement. Some proposals would add a different requirement by addressing the information a system supplies or how a device is installed.

  • New York S6197 / A3536 would require GPS systems to provide legal clearance information along proposed routes. The Senate record lists S6197 in committee.
  • Federal H.R. 6531 is a proposal concerning commercial vehicle routing and bridge strikes.
  • New Jersey A590 would allow mounting a GPS device where it does not unduly obstruct the driver's vision. The introduced text addresses device placement.

Assessing effects on recommended routes requires evidence about whether a particular restriction is in force, the road segment and time window it covers, and whether an app reflects it. The screening did not measure app behavior or traffic volumes. The News ↔ Law Crosswalk gives the related news evidence and the status of each linked instrument. A link to a proposal or an invalidated ordinance is evidence of the policy discussion.
