Section 1

Three-Layer Governance Framework Architecture

The project classifies all US traffic management, routing, and mobility instruments into a hierarchical three-layer framework based on their directness of relationship to navigation applications and digital routing platforms.

124 Codified Instruments · 100% Verified
Layer 1: Direct App Regulation 6 Provisions (4.8%)

Direct Platform Governance

Definition: The statutory or regulatory text explicitly names navigation apps, GPS routing, routing providers, digital mapping services, or their algorithmic route outputs, imposing an affirmative obligation, reporting charter, disclaimer mandate, or direct civil liability on them.

Benchmark Enacted / Pending Instruments:
  • • California AB 2015 (Ch. 207): Caltrans navigation app study & data exchange charter (Sts. & Hy. Code § 155.8).
  • • New Jersey A3036: SAFER APP Act mandating incorporation of municipal road restrictions with $1k/violation civil penalties.
  • • Connecticut HB 5071: Restricting commercial vehicles on parkways and requiring GPS route notifications.
  • • Federal H.R. 6531 (119th Cong.): Bridges Not Bumpers Act requiring bridge-clearance data integration in commercial GPS units.
  • • New York S6197: Electronic mapping and GPS navigation bridge clearance disclosure mandate.
Constitutional: 1st Amend. & Commerce Clause
Layer 2: Commercial & Hazmat 81 Provisions (65.3%)

Commercial Route Rules

Definition: Laws not specific to apps by name, but directly applicable to routing engines when recommending commercial routes. Covers commercial freight routing, truck gross weight exclusions (>6,000 lbs), National Network access, and hazardous materials corridors.

Benchmark Codified Instruments:
  • • Federal 49 CFR Part 397: Mandatory routing requirements for non-radioactive hazardous materials (NRHM).
  • • Federal 23 CFR Part 658 & 23 U.S.C. §127: National Network truck size and weight standards and reasonable access rules.
  • • Cal. Veh. Code §35701: Universal statutory police power enabling cities/counties to exclude vehicles >6k lbs.
  • • Virginia Code § 46.2-809: CTB regulation of through-truck traffic on primary and secondary highways.
  • • 58 California County Truck Codes: Codified commercial freight corridors across all 58 California counties.
Constitutional: FAAAA Preemption (§ 14501)
Layer 3: Indirect Traffic Laws 37 Provisions (29.8%)

Indirect Road & Traffic Laws

Definition: Laws governing physical road use, intersection turn restrictions, street closures, modal filters, speed limits, parking permits, and tolling. Navigation apps are not named, but act as passive cost-distance calculators that must route around these physical constraints.

Benchmark Codified Instruments:
  • • Cal. Veh. Code § 21101(f): Delegated local authority to prohibit turning movements to implement traffic plans.
  • • Cal. Veh. Code § 21101.6: Prohibition on selective gates/devices restricting through traffic (codifying Lafayette).
  • • VDOT Cut-Through Policy (24 VAC 30-151): Administrative framework for county peak-hour turn restrictions.
  • • 75 Pa.C.S. § 6109(a)(9): Pennsylvania statutory power authorizing municipal turn restrictions at intersections.
  • • Municipal Modal Filters: Bollards, diverters, and Slow Streets in SF, Los Angeles, Berkeley, and Seattle.
Constitutional: State Preemption & Rumford

Section 5

Comparative Regulatory Framework & Empirical Policy Analysis

Connecting the 121 statutory records to foundational enabling models, historical regulatory lag, and California's municipal policy alignment informing Assembly Bill 2015.

Model 1: Affirmative Statutory & Administrative

Cut-Through & Heavy Truck Policies

Jurisdictions that enacted dedicated statutes or state administrative frameworks granting local bodies explicit authority to restrict through-traffic or heavy vehicles upon engineering studies.

• Virginia: Va. Code § 46.2-809 (Through-truck rules) & VDOT Cut-Through Policy (24 VAC 30-151)
• Pennsylvania: 75 Pa.C.S. § 6109(a)(7) & (9) (Turn & Class exclusions)
Provides strongest legal defense against arbitrary/capricious challenges.
Model 2: Delegated Closed-List / Preemption

State Preemption & Enumerated Powers

States where local traffic regulation is preempted unless the municipality fits strictly within an enumerated statutory subdivision under strict judicial construction.

• California: CVC § 21101(f), § 21101.6 & § 35701
• Doctrine: Rumford v. Berkeley (1982); Lafayette (1979)
Limits cities to turn bans (§ 21101(f)) and truck routes (§ 35701); selective gates prohibited (§ 21101.6).
Model 3: Centralized State Approval

Mandatory State DOT Pre-Approval

Municipalities cannot alter traffic circulation or close streets connecting to state routes without prior formal review and written approval by the State DOT Commissioner.

• New Jersey: N.J.S.A. § 39:4-8 & § 39:4-197
• Precedent: Rosa v. Borough of Leonia (2018)
Unilateral local permit bans held ultra vires without State Commissioner sign-off.

Regulatory Lag: 20th-Century Physical Codes vs. 21st-Century Algorithmic Navigation

1959 — 2026

A core finding of this legal analysis is the chronological divergence between statutory architecture and digital navigation. Most state vehicle codes governing street access were enacted between 1959 and 1982 to address static, physical congestion. In contrast, modern third-party routing applications recalculate individual-optimal travel times dynamically every 30 seconds, rerouting hundreds of vehicles onto local streets.

The Pre-Digital Legal Baseline (1959–1991)
  • • Cal. Veh. Code § 35701 (1959): Focuses entirely on gross vehicle weight (curbing heavy trucks on local streets to protect pavement).
  • • City of Lafayette (1979) & CVC § 21101.6: Preempted selective gates and resident-only street closures, barring cities from closing roads to through commuters.
  • • Rumford v. City of Berkeley (1982): Preempted municipal physical diverters, establishing that the State owns all public streets.
  • • City of Poway (1991) (229 Cal. App. 3d 847): Banned closing roads connecting adjoining municipalities to prevent regional traffic dumping.
The Algorithmic Response Era (2014–2026)
  • • 2014–2018 Municipal Turn Bans: Cities adapt § 21101(f) to erect peak-hour turn signs (LAMC § 80.14) to break navigation app routing algorithms.
  • • 2018 Leonia Permit Ban (Rosa v. Leonia): Attempt to close 60 residential streets to non-residents struck down under pre-digital Title 39 statutory limits.
  • • 2026 California AB 2015 (Ch. 207): The first state statute specifically mandating a comprehensive pilot study to modernize the regulatory framework for routing applications (Sts. & Hy. Code § 155.8).
