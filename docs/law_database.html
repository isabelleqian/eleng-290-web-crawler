Enumerated Police Power

Model 3: Centralized Approval

State DOT Pre-Approval Regimes

• New Jersey: N.J.S.A. § 39:4-8
• Precedent: State v. Leonia (2018)
Unilateral local permit bans held ultra vires without State Commissioner sign-off.

Regulatory Lag: 20th-Century Physical Codes vs. 21st-Century Algorithmic Navigation

1959 — 2026

A core finding of this legal analysis is the chronological divergence between statutory architecture and digital navigation. Most state vehicle codes governing street access were enacted between 1959 and 1982 to address static, physical congestion. In contrast, modern third-party routing applications recalculate individual-optimal travel times dynamically every 30 seconds, rerouting hundreds of vehicles onto local streets.

The Pre-Digital Legal Baseline (1959–1982)
  • • Cal. Veh. Code § 35701 (1959): Focuses entirely on gross vehicle weight (curbing heavy trucks on local streets).
  • • Rumford v. City of Berkeley (1982): Preempted municipal physical diverters, assuming traffic flows along static public grids.
  • • City of Poway (1991): Restricts closing roads that connect adjoining municipalities to prevent regional traffic dumping.
The Algorithmic Response Era (2014–2026)
  • • 2014–2018 Municipal Turn Bans: Cities adapt § 21101(f) to erect peak-hour turn signs (LAMC § 80.14, Fremont § 10.05.780) to break navigation app routing algorithms.
  • • 2018 Leonia Permit Ban: Attempt to restrict public roads to non-residents struck down under pre-digital statutory limits.
  • • 2026 California AB 2015: The first state statute specifically mandating a comprehensive pilot study to modernize the regulatory framework for routing applications.
