# Navigation App Regulation: Three-Layer Summary (US, Phase 1 & 2)
**Research As-Of Date:** November 3, 2026  
**Notice:** Current status, amendments, and codified texts must be confirmed on official government legislative or regulatory websites prior to legal reliance.

This executive research summary classifies United States statutes, administrative regulations, and legislative instruments governing traffic management, routing, and vehicular mobility into the project **Three-Layer Governance Framework** (**Layer 1: Direct (App-Targeted)**, **Layer 2: Commercial (Commercial Vehicle Rules)**, and **Layer 3: Indirect (Road & Traffic Laws)**)**, **Layer 2: Route-Linked (Vehicle & Road Restrictions)**, and **Layer 3: Indirect (Road Conditions, Pricing & Planning)**) and **Six-Category Codebook** (Categories A through F). Full records, verbatim text offsets, official government links, and Section 8 liability findings are cataloged in the accompanying 156-record database.

---

# Layer 1: Direct (Laws directly governing navigation apps)
*Statutes and legislative proposals that explicitly name navigation applications, GPS routing providers, digital mapping platforms, or algorithmic route outputs, imposing affirmative routing restrictions, data reporting charters, or direct civil liability.*

* **E1 Routing restrictions for designated streets and slow streets**
  * **Enacted:** California AB 2015 (Ch. 207, Stats. 2026). Adds Cal. Sts. & Hy. Code § 155.8. Empowers localities with slow-streets programs to request navigation apps to exclude those streets from commuter cut-through routes.
  * **Failed:** Connecticut HB 5071 (2025). Would bar navigation apps from routing heavy commercial trucks onto local roads; dead in committee at session end.
  * **Stalled:** Massachusetts HD.4949 (2025–2026). Heavy-vehicle routing navigation restriction; in House Rules since July 2025.

* **E2 Official traffic restriction recognition and emergency rerouting**
  * **Enacted:** New Jersey A3036 / P.L. 2026, c. 12 (Smart App Freight Enforcement and Rerouting — SAFER APP Act). Empowers the Attorney General to order turn-by-turn navigation apps to reroute traffic in emergencies; imposes civil penalties up to $1,000 per violation; signed January 20, 2026.

* **E3 Route guidance and commercial vehicle routing criteria**
  * **Pending:** New York A4167 and A5717 (2025–2026 Regular Session). Mandates commercial GPS navigation units for commercial motor vehicles, trucks, and buses with low-clearance warnings; in Assembly Transportation since January 2026.
  * **Pending:** Federal H.R. 6531, Bridges Not Bumpers Act of 2025. Establishes USDOT interagency working group and national low-clearance clearinghouse; mandates commercial GPS integration; introduced December 9, 2025.

* **E4 Routing information correction and updating (bridge clearance)**
  * **Pending:** New York S6197 / A3536 (2025–2026 Regular Session). Requires electronic mapping and GPS navigation apps to prominently display legal bridge clearance limits; in Senate Consumer Protection and Assembly Consumer Affairs.

* **E6 Legal action and liability standards against routing providers**
  * **Substituted:** New Jersey S756 (2024–2025 Regular Session). Senate companion to SAFER APP establishing liability frameworks; substituted by A3036.

* **F3 Routing-impact monitoring and traffic analysis**
  * **Enacted:** California AB 2015 (see E1). Directs Caltrans, in consultation with CalSTA, to conduct a comprehensive empirical study of third-party navigation apps and their effects on state highway corridors and local streets.

# Layer 2: Commercial (Laws applicable to apps through commercial vehicle rules)
*Laws not naming apps specifically, but governing commercial freight routing, truck gross weight restrictions, National Network access, and hazardous materials corridors. Route providers must respect these limits when generating commercial navigation routes.*

* **A5 Vehicle-based restrictions: commercial routing standards**
  * **In force:** 23 CFR Part 658 (FED-06). National Network for truck size and weight; reasonable access provisions within 1 road-mile of the National Network.
  * **In force:** 23 U.S.C. § 127 (FED-06 statutory basis). Federal Interstate vehicle weight limitations and bridge formula.
  * **In force:** 49 CFR Part 397, Subparts C–D (FED-10). Hazardous materials routing; binds motor carriers and commercial drivers to state routing designations.
  * **In force:** 49 U.S.C. § 5112 (FED-11). Federal statutory authority for highway routing of hazardous material and federal preemption standards.

* **A5 Truck size and weight statutory changes**
  * **Enacted:** Alabama SB 110 / Act 2025-212 (AL-01). Truck weight exemptions and portable scale enforcement procedures; enacted May 6, 2025; effective October 1, 2025.
  * **Failed:** Federal H.R. 3372 (118th Congress, FED-12). 91,000-pound six-axle truck weight pilot program; not enacted. A similar pilot was added to a 2026 House highway bill in committee (unverified).

* **A5 State commercial route enabling acts and municipal truck codes**
  * **In force:** California Vehicle Code § 35701 (CA-35701). Core state enabling act empowering local authorities to designate truck routes and exclude commercial vehicles (>6,000 lbs) from residential streets.
  * **In force:** Virginia Code Ann. § 46.2-809 (VA-02). Through-truck prohibitions on primary and secondary highways upon engineering studies.
  * **In force:** Pennsylvania 75 Pa.C.S. § 6109(a)(7),(9) (PA-01). Local vehicle class exclusions and bridge weight limits.
  * **In force:** Massachusetts Gen. Laws ch. 85, § 2 (MA-01). Heavy vehicle exclusions on municipal ways.
  * **In force:** New Jersey Stat. Ann. § 39:4-197 (NJ-02). Municipal truck weight and route exclusions under state police powers.
  * **In force:** New York Veh. & Traf. Law § 385 (NY-01). Commercial vehicle legal dimensions, height (13.5 ft), and gross weight limits.
  * **In force:** Oakland Municipal Code Title 10, Chapter 52 (OK-03). Commercial vehicles and truck routes.
  * **In force:** 58 California County Truck Codes (CTY-01 through CTY-58). Enabling commercial routes and weight limits under CVC § 35701.

* *Note:* F1, F2, and F6 (data exchange, government-platform pilots, traveler information) have no standalone mandatory entries in Phase 1 and are not covered in this layer.

# Layer 3: Indirect (Indirectly applicable road and traffic laws)
*General road rules, physical closures, intersection turn prohibitions, residential parking permits, automated speed enforcement, tolling, and planning mandates. Apps are affected parties or passive calculators that must compute routes compliant with these physical and legal network constraints.*

* **A1 Turn restrictions**
  * **In force:** California Vehicle Code § 22101 (CA-01). Local authority to regulate turning movements at intersections by signs or markers.
  * **In force:** California Vehicle Code § 22113 (CA-02). Local authority to prohibit turns at designated intersections.
  * **In force:** California MUTCD 2026, CVC § 21400 (CA-08). Standards for turn and traffic control signs and markings.
  * **In force:** Berkeley BMC § 14.16.030 (BK-01). Left-turn restrictions between intersections.
  * **In force:** Florida Stat. § 316.008 (FL-01) & Coral Gables Code § 74-120 (FL-02). Peak-hour turn bans.
  * **In force:** Georgia Code Ann. § 40-6-371 (GA-01); Maryland Transp. § 25-102 (MD-01); Colorado C.R.S. § 42-4-111 (CO-01); Connecticut C.G.S. § 14-298 (CT-01) & Greenwich Code § 14-15 (CT-02).

* **A2 Through-traffic restrictions and street closures**
  * **In force:** California Vehicle Code § 21101 (CA-09). Local closures and traffic rules by ordinance or resolution (§ 21101(a) closures, § 21101(f) slow streets).
  * **In force:** California AB 773 (CA-04). Slow streets designations and closures; amends CVC § 21101(f).
  * **In force:** California Public Resources Code § 21080.25(b) (CA-13). CEQA statutory exemption for slow streets ordinances.
  * **In force:** Oakland Slow Streets (OK-05). Neighborhood bike routes closed to through traffic under emergency powers.
  * **In force:** Town of Vienna Code § 8-40 (VA-01). Residential cut-through traffic prohibitions.

* **A4 Permit-based access and exemptions**
  * **In force:** California Vehicle Code § 22507 (CA-03). Residential preferential parking permits (local authority).
  * **In force:** Berkeley Preferential Parking Program (BK-05). BMC Chapter 14.72, § 14.80.030 (BK-06), § 14.76.030 Bateman area (BK-07).
  * **In force:** Oakland Residential Permit Parking (OK-01). OMC Chapter 10.44 & Area P establishment (OK-02).
  * **Invalidated (Court Precedent):** Borough of Leonia Ordinance No. 2017-18 (NJ-01). Peak-hour resident-only street closures struck down in court as ultra vires under Title 39.

* **A5 Vehicle- and trip-purpose-based restrictions (local residential)**
  * **In force:** California Vehicle Code § 22507.5 (CA-11). Commercial vehicle parking restrictions in residential districts.
  * **In force:** Berkeley BMC § 14.56.060 (BK-02) truck route system, § 14.56.070 (BK-03) residential streets closed to trucks over 3 tons, and § 14.56.040 (BK-04) Marin Ave commercial ban.
  * **In force:** Oakland OMC § 10.52.060 (OK-04). Streets closed to commercial vehicles over 4.5 tons.

* **B1 Traffic filters, diverters, and modal closures**
  * **In force where federal funds or approval apply:** NEPA review, 42 U.S.C. § 4321 et seq. (FED-04).
  * **In force:** Seattle Municipal Code § 11.16.120 (WA-03). Home Zones and Neighborhood Greenways physical modal filters.
  * **In force:** Washington RCW 47.52.025 (WA-02); Illinois 625 ILCS 5/11-208 (IL-01); Texas Transp. Code § 542.202 (TX-02); New York VTL § 1640 (NY-02); New Jersey N.J.S.A. § 39:4-8 (NJ-03); DC 18 DCMR § 2205 (DC-01).

* **B3 Street narrowing and accessibility of pedestrian facilities**
  * **Partly in force:** Public Right-of-Way Accessibility Guidelines (PROWAG) under ADA Title II, 42 U.S.C. § 12131 et seq. (FED-02). USDOT transit stop standards in effect; DOJ adoption pending.

* **C1 Traffic signal control and red-light enforcement**
  * **In force:** Texas Transportation Code Chapter 707 (TX-01). Prohibits red-light photo enforcement cameras statewide; automated citations void.

* **C4 Automated speed enforcement and speed limits**
  * **In force:** Arkansas Act 707 of 2023 (AR-01). Automated work-zone speed cameras, effective August 1, 2023.
  * **In force:** Washington RCW 46.63.220 / ESHB 2384 (WA-01). School zone, park, and highway corridor cameras, effective June 6, 2024.
  * **Enacted:** Colorado SB 26-152 (CO-02). Speed camera standards with 6 mph fine threshold; signed June 2, 2026.
  * **Approved by voters:** Arizona Proposition 319 (AZ-01). Bans new photo enforcement unless contracted by Dec. 31, 2026; canvass pending.
  * **In force:** California AB 645 Speed Safety System Pilot Program (CA-05 / OK-06 in Oakland) and CVC § 22358 / AB 43 engineering surveys (CA-10).
  * **Vetoed:** Arizona SB 1624 (AZ-02, vetoed June 19, 2026); Arizona SB 1019 (AZ-03, vetoed July 1, 2025).

* **C5 Dynamic lane and roadway allocation**
  * **In force:** 23 U.S.C. § 166 (FED-09). Federal statutory rules for HOV and HOT lane dynamic allocation and occupancy standards.

* **D2 Road or facility tolling**
  * **In force:** 23 U.S.C. § 129 (FED-07). Federal tolling agreements on federal-aid highways, bridges, and tunnels.

* **D3 Dynamic, time-, or distance-based pricing**
  * **In force:** Berkeley BMC Chapter 14.52, § 14.52.120 (BK-08). Demand-responsive goBerkeley parking meter rates.
  * **In process:** Oakland OakPark+ demand-responsive parking pricing (OK-07).

* **F4 Integration into transportation and land-use planning**
  * **In force:** Metropolitan transportation planning, 23 U.S.C. §§ 134–135; 23 CFR Part 450 (FED-03).
  * **In force:** Clean Air Act transportation conformity, 42 U.S.C. § 7506(c) (FED-05).
  * **In force:** California Complete Streets Act / General Plan Circulation Element, Gov. Code § 65302(b) / AB 1358 (CA-07).
  * **In force:** California SB 743: Vehicle miles traveled as CEQA transportation metric, PRC § 21099 (CA-12).

* **F5 Transportation demand management and mode shift**
  * **In force:** 23 U.S.C. § 149, Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality Improvement (CMAQ) program (FED-08).
  * **In force:** BAAQMD Regulation 13, Rule 1: Trip Reduction Requirements for Large Employers (RG-01).
  * **In force:** Berkeley Trip Reduction Information Program, BMC Chapter 14.92 (BK-09).

* **F7 Development and subdivision design requirements**
  * **In force:** Berkeley Transportation Demand Management for development, BMC Chapter 23.334 (BK-10).
  * **In force:** California Government Code § 65089.3, trip reduction and congestion management ordinances (CA-06).
