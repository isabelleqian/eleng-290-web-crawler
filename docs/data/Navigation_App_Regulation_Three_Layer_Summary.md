# Navigation App Regulation: Three-Layer Summary (US, Phase 1)
**Research As-Of Date:** November 3, 2026  
**Notice:** Current status, amendments, and codified texts must be confirmed on official government legislative or regulatory websites prior to legal reliance.

This executive research summary classifies United States statutes, administrative regulations, and legislative instruments governing traffic management, routing, and vehicular mobility into the project Three-Layer Governance Framework and Six-Category Codebook. Full records, verbatim text offsets, and character boundaries are cataloged in the accompanying database.

---

# Layer 1: Laws Directly Governing Navigation Apps
*Statutory provisions and legislative instruments that explicitly name navigation applications, GPS routing providers, digital mapping services, or algorithm route outputs, or impose affirmative statutory obligations, study charters, or civil liability.*

* **E1: Routing restrictions for designated streets or areas**
  * **California** — Enacted (signed Sept 22, 2026): Cal. Stats. 2026, Ch. 207 (Assembly Bill 2015, Caltrans Third-Party Routing Study) [ID: CA-03]
  * **New Jersey** — Bill – Pending (referred to Assembly Transportation Committee Jan 9, 2024): NJ A3036 (Smart App Freight Enforcement and Rerouting / SAFER APP Act) [ID: NJ-01]
    * *Sub-bullet (Pending):* NJ S2322 (companion Senate bill establishing civil penalty of up to $1,000 per violation for routing providers directing commercial carriers onto restricted local roads)

* **E3: Route guidance and routing criteria**
  * **Connecticut** — Bill – Pending (referred to Joint Committee on Transportation Jan 15, 2025): CT HB 5071 (Commercial Motor Vehicles on Parkways & GPS Navigation Disclaimers) [ID: CT-01]
    * *Sub-bullet (Stalled/Pending):* CT SB 338 (prohibiting passenger-car navigation app routing for heavy motor vehicles on Merritt and Wilbur Cross Parkways)

* **E4: Routing information correction and updating**
  * **New York** — Bill – Pending (referred to Senate Transportation Committee Jan 2023): 2023 NY Senate Bill S6197 (Electronic Mapping Application Updates for Municipal Street Closures) [ID: NY-02]
    * *Sub-bullet (Pending):* NY Assembly Bill A4167 (requiring navigation app platforms to ingest local temporary turn restrictions and weight closures within 48 hours of municipal transmission)

# Layer 2: Laws Applicable Through Commercial Vehicle & Hazardous Materials Routing
*Statutory provisions and administrative regulations not specific to consumer digital applications, but legally binding upon routing providers and motor carriers through mandatory commercial vehicle routing, gross vehicle weight caps, National Network access, or hazardous materials routing.*

* **A5: Vehicle- or trip-purpose-based access restrictions (Commercial Truck Routes)**
  * **Federal (United States)** — In force: 49 CFR § 397.67 / 49 U.S.C. § 5112 (Motor Carrier Responsibilities for Hazardous Materials Routing) [ID: FED-01]
  * **Federal (United States)** — In force: 23 CFR § 658.19 (National Network Reasonable Access for Commercial Motor Vehicles) [ID: FED-04]
  * **Federal (United States)** — In force: 23 U.S.C. § 127 (Vehicle Weight Limitations on the Interstate Highway System) [ID: FED-02]
  * **California** — In force: Cal. Veh. Code § 35701 (Municipal Authority to Restrict Commercial Vehicles >6,000 lbs and Designate Truck Routes) [ID: CA-01]
  * **New Jersey** — In force: N.J. Stat. Ann. § 39:4-197.16 (County & Municipal Commercial Truck Route Designations) [ID: NJ-02]
  * **New York** — In force: N.Y. Veh. & Traf. Law § 385 (Dimensions and Weights of Vehicles & Local Truck Routes) [ID: NY-01]
  * **Massachusetts** — In force: Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 85, § 2 (Municipal Regulation of Heavy Commercial Vehicles) [ID: MA-01]
  * *California County Coverage (Phase 2):* 58 California County Code chapters establishing commercial vehicle routing and weight restrictions (Alameda County Title 10 through Yuba County Title 9).

# Layer 3: Indirectly Applicable Road and Traffic Laws
*General statutory and municipal laws governing public road use, intersection turn movements, physical traffic calming, speed management, through-traffic bans, parking, and tolling. Navigation applications are affected parties or passive network followers whose cost-distance routing algorithms respond to these legal constraints.*

* **A1: Turn restrictions at intersections**
  * **California** — In force: Cal. Veh. Code § 21101(f) (Delegated Municipal Authority to Prohibit Turning Movements) [ID: CA-02]
  * **Florida** — In force: Fla. Stat. § 316.008(1)(g) (Powers of Local Authorities to Prohibit Turning Movements at Intersections) [ID: FL-01]
  * **Connecticut** — In force: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 14-298 (State Traffic Commission Jurisdiction over Direction of Turns) [ID: CT-02]
  * **Colorado** — In force: Colo. Rev. Stat. § 42-4-110 (Powers of Local Authorities to Regulate Turning Movements) [ID: CO-01]
  * **Maryland** — In force: Md. Code Ann., Transp. § 25-102 (Local Powers to Prohibit Turning Movements) [ID: MD-01]
  * *Representative Municipal Sections:* City of Los Angeles (LAMC § 80.14), City of Fremont (FMC § 10.05.780), City of Beverly Hills (BHMC § 4-2.1001), City of Mountain View (MVCC § 19.34).

* **A2: Through-traffic restrictions (Cut-Through Street Acts)**
  * **Virginia** — In force: Va. Code Ann. § 46.2-808.1 (Local Authority to Designate Residential Cut-Through Traffic Restrictions) [ID: VA-01]
  * **Pennsylvania** — In force: 75 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 6109(a)(11) (Municipal Authority to Regulate or Prohibit Through Traffic) [ID: PA-01]
  * **Virginia (Municipal)** — In force: Town of Vienna Code § 8-40 (Through-Traffic Prohibition on Residential Streets) [ID: VA-02]

* **A4: Permit-based access and exemptions**
  * **New Jersey (Municipal)** — Struck down as ultra vires (2018): Borough of Leonia Ordinance No. 2017-18 (Resident-Only Rush Hour Street Access Permits) [ID: NJ-03]
    * *Judicial Holding:* State v. Borough of Leonia (App. Div. 2018) invalidated non-resident rush-hour closures without formal N.J. DOT Commissioner sign-off.

* **B1: Traffic filters, diverters, and physical closures**
  * **California (Municipal)** — In force: S.F. Transportation Code Div. II, § 601 (SFMTA Board Authority for Traffic Diverters & Slow Streets) [ID: CA-SF-01]
  * **California (Municipal)** — In force: Berkeley Municipal Code § 14.32.010 (Physical Barriers and Diverters) [ID: CA-BERK-01]
  * **Washington (Municipal)** — In force: Seattle Municipal Code § 11.16.120 (Director of Transportation Authority for Neighborhood Traffic Calming) [ID: WA-02]

* **D1: Area and cordon congestion charging**
  * **New York** — In force / Pending implementation: N.Y. Veh. & Traf. Law § 1704-a (Central Business District Tolling Program) [ID: NY-03]
  * **Federal (United States)** — Bill – Failed (died in committee at end of 118th Congress): H.R. 6531 (Bridges Not Bumpers Act) [ID: FED-05]

# Notes on Scope, Coverage, and Verification
1. **Jurisdictional Coverage:**
   - **Federal Level:** Fully searched and verified (Title 23 U.S.C., Title 49 U.S.C., Title 23 CFR, Title 49 CFR).
   - **Priority State Jurisdictions (Phase 1):** California, Connecticut, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Texas, Virginia, Washington, and District of Columbia.
   - **California Local Jurisdictions (Phase 2):** 58 California Counties (complete county code coverage) and 15 granular municipal codes (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Berkeley, Fremont, Oakland, San Jose, San Diego, Beverly Hills, Burbank, Palo Alto, Mountain View, Pasadena, Santa Monica, Irvine, Glendale).
   - **Standard States (Remaining 36 States):** State-level legislative tracking conducted; states lacking direct Layer 1 or Layer 2 routing legislation rely on Uniform Vehicle Code delegated powers (cataloged as gap/no_direct_statute).
2. **Verification Standard:**
   - Enacted statutes and chaptered laws carry official citations verified against state legislative websites (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, uscode.house.gov, njleg.state.nj.us, cga.ct.gov, nysenate.gov).
   - Unenacted bills carry exact status dates and chamber committee assignments.
3. **Database Consistency:**
   - Every instrument cited in this Three-Layer Summary corresponds to a unique persistent ID in `accepted_legislation.csv`, `layer_map.csv`, and `detailed_database_19_col.csv`.
