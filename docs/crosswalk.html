Remaining Legal Gaps in California (AB 2015 Empirical News Synthesis)

Detailed legal synthesis of 52 California news records (Los Angeles Council Files 14-1741 & 11-2130, Baxter Street, LAPD, San Francisco SFMTA, and Caltrans) against the California Vehicle Code and appellate precedent.

What California Law Authorizes (Existing Police Powers) Enacted Baseline
  • 1. Peak-Hour Turn Bans (Cal. Veh. Code § 21101(f))

    LADOT and Bay Area cities can legally install turn restrictions at arterial-collector intersections (e.g. Sherman Oaks, Silver Lake), provided they conduct mandatory, rigorous engineering and traffic surveys proving safety necessity.

  • 2. Permanent "Slow Streets" Designation (AB 773 / CVC § 21101)

    Enacted legislation (AB 773) authorizes SFMTA, Oakland, and LA to designate permanent residential slow streets prioritizing pedestrians and cyclists without declaring an emergency.

  • 3. Mounted Hands-Free Device Exception (CVC §§ 23123 & 23123.5)

    California explicitly protects vehicular navigation by exempting dashboard-mounted, voice-operated smartphone GPS usage from distracted driving prohibitions.

  • 4. TNC Dispatch & Telemetry Regulation (Cal. Pub. Util. Code §§ 5430–5450)

    CPUC exercises direct statewide regulatory jurisdiction over rideshare fleets (Uber/Lyft), but its jurisdiction does not extend to private passenger vehicular navigation platforms like Google Maps or Apple Maps.

Critical Unaddressed Legal Gaps Remaining in California Statutory Voids
  • GAP 1: The AB 2015 Scope Void (Study vs. Substantive Authority)

    California AB 2015 (Buffy Wicks) directs Caltrans and CalSTA to conduct an empirical pilot study (report due Jan 1, 2028). Crucially, AB 2015 grants zero rulemaking, regulatory, or enforcement powers to compel mapping apps to alter routing weights or respect slow street exclusions.
