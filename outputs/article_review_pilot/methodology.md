## Reproducible Nexis selection
The initial frame contained 1,592 readable files and 1,592 unique document IDs. Starting with lexicographically sorted `data/nexis/**/readable_article.txt` paths, read sibling `metadata.json`, build the existing importer identifier as `nexis_` + `document_id`, and retain the first path per ID. Sort by `(publication_date[:10], source_id)`. For each i from 0 through 9, take slice `[floor(N*i/10):floor(N*(i+1)/10)]`. Within the band prefer publishers not already selected, compared using Python `casefold()`; use all band members if none qualify. Select the minimum hexadecimal SHA256 of the UTF-8 string `article-review-pilot-v1|SOURCE_ID`. No random generator, relevance screen or discretionary replacement was used.

The selection CSV preserves the ten IDs, starting titles, publisher labels, sampling dates and readable paths. It spans 2012–2026 with ten different publisher labels. Dates from metadata define sampling only; they are not automatically accepted as verified publication dates. The algorithm is reproducible against the same archive state; the saved selection fixes this pilot even if that archive later changes. This is a coverage pilot, not a representative statistical sample. The two examples were reviewed first; the user then authorized the remaining records.

## Actual review method
Read each ordinary article or post, then use public URLs, exact-title searches, distinctive passages, author/date queries, publisher indexes and browser inspection to check identity. Source XML supplied the original Nexar and American Banker links. Browser Google searches were used when web-search results did not resolve the Crain, Ridgefield and Elk Grove records. A matching event alone was insufficient. For example, Beaumont reused a headline; the December 2025 article was rejected in favor of the April 21, 2026 body matching this Nexis record. Generic launch coverage and annual reports were not substituted for the Elk Grove release.

For long documents, inspect identity/contents and read the selected relevant section, rather than claiming to review every page of an entire dissertation or packet. Sections used: Seattle outreach report pp. 2–5; SLNC submission and statement PDF pp. 1–3; Berkeley cover and abstract PDF pp. 1, 4–5; NAP Chapter 8 pp. 56–68; Mantoloking Barnegat Lane caucus discussion pp. 1–4; Smarking commitment letter in Smart City Challenge Volume 2 pp. 108–111; SFCTA item 5, Slow Streets request pp. 99–101, toolkit pp. 108–110 and Sanchez request pp. 117–119. Page references are printed unless labeled PDF. No source was excluded simply for being a government page, PDF, forum, press release, opinion piece or packet.

Treat article text plus selected metadata as the intended readable-file format. Do not comment on this packaging in review rows. Flag substantive identity mismatches, missing/incomplete content or unintended mixtures that affect a reliable summary. Deliberate collections and packets are scoped explicitly. Live pages can change: Lake City now describes later pilot phases; its summary preserves the archived plan and notes the version difference.

## Summary instructions
Use about 70–100 words where warranted (original pilot summaries: 67–86 words). Summarize the selected archived article/section: who acted, what happened, where and when, only as supported. Distinguish proposals, approvals, requirements, rollout and completed work. Attribute reported claims, forecasts and opinions; do not turn projected emissions, congestion or safety benefits into measured results. Exclude search/Nexis metadata, navigation, related articles and unsupported interpretation. Summaries are grounded in archived text; public evidence checks identity and metadata without silently updating the historical account. The letters collection summarizes each writer separately; promotional releases and commentary are labeled and attributed.
